## Context

- I am an analyst on the **Security Awareness Team**.
- The Chief Security Officer relies on us to help staff at Mastercard learn to identify and report security threats.
- One of the most common threats to organizations is **phishing**.

> [!quote] Phishing
> - Phishing is the act of pretending to be someone/something to get information, in most cases, this is usually a password.
> -  Attackers may send links or attachments designed to infect the recipient's system with malicious software or lure them into providing financial information, system credentials or other sensitive data.

- Successful phishing attempts can cost companies like Mastercard millions of dollars and put our employees at risk. So it’s very important that we keep the business and our staff safe from harm.
- So, in order to mitigate phishing threats, we test our staff by sending a fake phishing email, every month, that is made to look like something a bad actor would send. The results are used to design and implement future training.

## What can a phishing email look like?

- Typo in the `from` email.
- Email domain is wrong.
- Subject line has things like "URGENT!"
- Email body is sloppily written and has placeholder text.
### Example Phishing email features

- Email comes from a relatable address
- Email is personalized
- Email is contextualised around a relatable event for the recipient 
- Links are masked to hide phishing link.
- Buffer text is added for legitimacy (For more enquiries contact...)
- Confidentiality disclaimer to add legitimacy
